### Aircraft lock (paste into EVERY prompt that shows the jet)
> Convair F-106A Delta Dart, 1970 USAF interceptor: TAILLESS DELTA WING (big triangular wing, NO horizontal stabilizers at all), one tall swept vertical fin with a SQUARED-OFF FLAT TOP, slim "coke-bottle" fuselage with a long pointed nose, bubble canopy, air intakes on both sides just behind the cockpit, single engine exhaust. Light Air Defense Command grey paint, small USAF star insignia, no readable text or numbers.

**Common mistakes to reject:**
- a horizontal tail (it is a tailless delta);
- a pointed fin top (the fin top is flat);
- intakes under the nose;
- two engines.

### Look (every prompt)
> Photorealistic, 1970 color film look, natural light, realistic, no text, no watermark, vertical 9:16.

## Script (for timing only, voice is made separately)

> This pilot just ejected. His jet didn't.
> February 1970. Montana. Lieutenant Gary Foust is in a training dogfight in an F-106 Delta Dart, when his jet falls into a flat spin. Nothing he does can stop it. At fifteen thousand feet, he pulls the handle and ejects.
> Then something strange happens. Without the pilot, the jet's balance changes. The nose drops, the spin stops, and the empty jet levels out and keeps flying.
> Hanging under his parachute, Foust hears another pilot on the radio: "Gary, you'd better get back in it!"
> Nobody is at the controls. The jet glides lower and lower over the snow, and settles onto its belly in a farmer's field. Almost gently.
> The engine is still running. The local sheriff arrives and calls the air base for instructions. When he climbs up to the cockpit, the jet starts sliding forward on the snow. So he climbs back down and waits for it to run out of fuel.
> The damage is so minor that the Air Force takes off the wings, ships it out by train, repairs it, and sends it back into service. Pilots call it the Cornfield Bomber.
> Nine years later, Gary Foust climbs into that same jet again. He finally got back in it.
> Today it sits in the National Museum of the United States Air Force. Some landings aren't in the manual.

## Shot list

**CLIP** = video, with first frame (F) and, where given, last frame (L) generated first with Nano Banana Pro, then Frames-to-video.
**IMG** = still image only (the montage adds camera moves).

### S01: HOOK (CLIP, 6 s), the most important shot

- **F:** High above snowy Montana plains on a clear frigid day. In the upper foreground a USAF pilot in a 1970 olive flight suit and white helmet hangs under a round orange-and-white parachute, seen from behind and slightly above, looking down. Far below him the F-106 (aircraft lock) flies straight and level on its own, canopy gone, clearly visible as a delta silhouette against the white snow, in the centre of the frame.
- **L:** Same scene, the pilot in the same place, the jet now smaller and further toward the lower-right, still level, its shadow on the snow.
- **Motion:** The pilot swings gently under the canopy; far below the empty jet keeps flying away, straight and level. Slow, eerie, calm. Static camera. No fire, no explosion.
- **Must:** pilot and jet both clearly readable in the very first frame, and motion from frame 1.

### S02: IMG
Two F-106s (aircraft lock) high over snow-covered Rocky Mountain front ranges, deep blue sky, white vapour trails curling as they turn toward each other in a mock dogfight.

### S03: CLIP (5 s), flat spin
- **F:** The F-106 (aircraft lock) seen from a chase plane above, spinning flat like a falling leaf, nose level, snowy ground far below.
- **L:** Same, rotated about 180°, lower.
- **Motion:** The jet rotates flat and sinks, slowly turning. No smoke, no fire.

### S04: IMG
Inside a 1970 jet-fighter cockpit, over the pilot's shoulder: gloved hands gripping the stick, red warning lights on a grey analog panel, the snowy horizon tilted and blurred through the canopy.

### S05: CLIP (4 s), ejection
- **F:** The F-106 (aircraft lock) from outside and slightly behind, canopy just separating.
- **Motion:** The canopy flies off and the ejection seat with the pilot shoots up out of the cockpit on a short rocket plume, snowy land far below. No explosion of the aircraft.

### S06: IMG
Close view from above and behind of the F-106 (aircraft lock) flying alone: the open cockpit is completely EMPTY, no canopy, no seat, no pilot; snowy plains below.

### S07: CLIP (5 s), recovery
- **F:** The empty F-106 (open, empty cockpit) tilted in the spin, nose high.
- **L:** The same jet flying straight and level, wings level, nose slightly down.
- **Motion:** The nose drops, the spin stops, the jet levels out and keeps flying on its own. Seen from a chase plane.

### S08: CLIP (5 s), "Gary, you'd better get back in it!"
- **F:** View from the cockpit of a second F-106 in formation: right alongside flies the F-106 (aircraft lock) with its cockpit open and EMPTY.
- **Motion:** Both jets fly steadily in formation; the empty jet bobs slightly. Snowy plains below.

### S09: IMG
Close-up side view: the pilot (1970 white helmet, olive flight suit) hanging under the orange-and-white parachute, looking down in disbelief; snowy mountains below, clear cold sky.

### S10: CLIP (6 s), belly landing
- **F:** Low side view: the empty F-106 (aircraft lock, open empty cockpit, landing gear UP) gliding a few metres above a flat snow-covered wheat field, fence posts, a line of bare trees and farm buildings on the horizon (use `F-106_unmanned_landing.jpg` as the scene reference).
- **L:** The jet resting on its belly in the snow, intact, gear up, a long furrow behind it, powder snow settling. Composition close to the real photo.
- **Motion:** The jet glides lower, touches the snow on its belly and slides to a smooth stop, throwing up soft powder snow. No crash, no fire, no smoke.
