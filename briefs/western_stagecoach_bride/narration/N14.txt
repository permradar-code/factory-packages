Amos Pruitt had been an express guard for thirty years. For two of them, he had been collecting proof.

Crane had a clerk in the Denver office who sent him the shipment dates. Crane wrote back. And the clerk, who was not a brave man but was a careful one, had kept every letter. When he finally lost his nerve, he gave them to the only honest man he knew. Amos was carrying them to the marshal in Cedar Bluff.

The gold had never been in the box at all. Crane had lied about that too. He had only needed a reason to hang the bride before anyone read what she was carrying.
