The tracks led north, around the town, through two creeks and across a stretch of bare rock where most men would have lost them. Mercer did not lose them.

They ended at a long row of horses at a hitching rail, in front of a white house with a wide veranda.

The house of Silas Crane.

That night, at the Whitmore kitchen table, Rose finally opened the second lock. Not with a key. With one of Clara's hairpins and a trick her father had taught her, back when he printed handbills for every locksmith in St. Louis.

Inside there was no gold at all. There was a stack of letters, tied with string, in a fine slanting hand.
